# Interoperability and contribution policy

OpenGOverlay aims solely to enable independently developed software to communicate with legitimately owned LCDSysInfo 3.5-inch hardware. It is an unofficial compatibility project, not a modification or redistribution of the vendor application.

## Research and publication boundaries

- Prefer public specifications, standard USB documentation and observation of lawful operation on authorized hardware. Record provenance and firmware versions.
- Before any further static analysis involving reproduction or translation of object code, verify lawful access, necessity, availability of the required information from public sources, and the applicable legal and contractual conditions. Limit analysis to the portions indispensable for interoperability.
- Publish only independently worded functional interface specifications and test results necessary for interoperability, after review. Keep internal working notes and unnecessary implementation details out of the public repository. Do not imply that information obtained by decompilation can freely be disclosed.
- Do not submit vendor code (including decompiled code or disassemblies), firmware, proprietary binaries, fonts, graphics, copied manuals, license keys, or other third-party material without an appropriate right to distribute it.
- Do not implement or document methods to circumvent activation, authentication, licensing, device locks, access controls or other protection mechanisms. Exclude unnecessary persistent-write and service-related functionality.
- Write original implementation code from reviewed functional specifications. Prefer independent observation and independent review; do not describe this process as a verified clean-room implementation.
- Do not access, probe or interfere with vendor servers. Do not publish device serials or personal data.
- Use product names only for identification and compatibility, not to imply endorsement. No vendor logos. Respect third-party rights and applicable licenses.
- Distinguish static inference from tested behavior. Avoid unsupported accusations or claims about vendor intent.

These references do not establish that every proposed activity is lawful. Interoperability exceptions have conditions and disclosure limits; other rights and applicable laws may still matter. Seek qualified legal review before a public implementation release or when the legal basis for a contribution is uncertain.
